# SEO (Search & Discovery) Audit — 2026-04-05

**Auditor:** AI lane (SEO audit process v1)
**Scope:** All public-facing routes in `app/app/`, `sitemap.ts`, `robots.ts`, root `layout.tsx`
**Out of scope:** Implementing fixes; detailed CWV remediation (handed off to Performance lane); authenticated app shell internals beyond indexability verification

---

## Executive summary

- **Crawling infrastructure is solid.** `robots.ts` correctly disallows all authenticated-app paths; `sitemap.ts` covers the full public URL tree; auth routes (`/sign-in`, `/sign-up`) and the app shell (`(app)/layout.tsx`) self-noindex with appropriate metadata. No accidental indexing of sensitive routes was found.
- **Metadata quality is strong on most pages** but has three systemic gaps: (1) all public pages share the same single root-level OG/Twitter image (no page-level overrides), meaning every social preview looks identical; (2) `sitemap.ts` hardcodes competitor alternative and vs slugs rather than reading them dynamically from the data source, so new pages could be silently omitted; (3) the pricing FAQ and other `<details>/<summary>` FAQ blocks have no `FAQPage` JSON-LD, missing a rich-result opportunity.
- **OG title/meta title mismatches** exist on several hub pages (`/tools`, `/vs`), creating inconsistent SERP/social signals.
- **No Search Console property verification or sitemap submission is documented** in the repo; this is an operational gap that can prevent Google from discovering and prioritizing the sitemap.

## Severity-ranked findings

### High

**F-H1 — Single shared OG/Twitter image across entire site**
All public pages (pricing, tools hub, each calculator, alternatives, vs, resources, changelog) inherit the root-level OG image `/og-image.png` from `layout.tsx`. No page sets its own `openGraph.images` or `twitter.images`. When any page is shared on LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Slack, or iMessage, the preview image is identical — generic branding only. This reduces click-through from social shares on high-intent pages like calculators and pricing.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/layout.tsx` lines 53–62 (root OG image); `app/app/pricing/page.tsx` (no images override); `app/app/tools/page.tsx` (no images override); same for all other public pages.
- **Risk:** Medium-high. Social distribution effectiveness is degraded; tools and pricing pages miss the biggest win from page-specific previews.

---

**F-H2 — `sitemap.ts` hardcodes alternative and vs slugs; new pages silently omitted**
`alternatives/stessa`, `alternatives/rentastic`, `alternatives/cozy`, `vs/spreadsheets`, and `vs/excel-rental-property` are written as literal strings. The actual slugs are driven by `COMPETITOR_ALTERNATIVES` and `COMPETITOR_VS` data objects (`lib/marketing/competitor-data.ts`). If a new competitor page is added to those data files, the sitemap won't include the URL until someone manually adds it here — while `resources/[slug]` correctly uses `getResourceSlugs()` dynamically.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/sitemap.ts` lines 51–91 (hardcoded alternatives/vs slugs) vs. lines 98–103 (dynamic resources).
- **Risk:** Medium. New comparison pages would be discoverable only through internal links, not sitemap submission.

---

**F-H3 — Pricing FAQ has rich Q&A content but no `FAQPage` JSON-LD**
The `/pricing` page renders four detailed Q&A pairs inside `<details>/<summary>` elements (and two more in the bottom CTA section). This content qualifies for Google's FAQ rich result, which can increase SERP space and click-through on a high-commercial-intent page. The pricing page emits no structured data at all; only the calculator pages (`/investment-property-calculator`, `/tools/brrr`, `/tools/str-vs-ltr`, `/tools/fix-and-flip`) use `CalculatorFaqJsonLd`.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/pricing/page.tsx` lines 222–249 (FAQ block), no JSON-LD import.
- **Risk:** Medium. Lost rich-result eligibility on a page with direct conversion value.

---

### Medium

**F-M1 — OG title/meta title mismatch on `/tools` hub**
The meta `title` is `"Free real estate calculators"` (becomes `"Free real estate calculators | Veld Portfolio"`), which is keyword-rich and well-targeted. But the `openGraph.title` is `"Calculators | Veld Portfolio"` — generic and inconsistent. When the tools hub is shared socially, the card shows a weaker title than what appears in Google SERPs.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/tools/page.tsx` lines 15–25.

---

**F-M2 — `/vs` hub: meta title "Compare" doesn't match H1 "Veld vs spreadsheets"**
The `metadata.title` is `"Compare"`, resolving to `"Compare | Veld Portfolio"` — highly generic and unlikely to attract organic clicks. The page's H1 reads `"Veld vs spreadsheets"`, which is more descriptive and keyword-aligned. The title and H1 should be coherent; here the meta title undersells the page intent.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/vs/page.tsx` lines 13–23 (metadata), line 47 (H1).

---

**F-M3 — `/alternatives` hub title exceeds ~60-char SERP display target with brand duplication**
`title: "Veld Alternatives — Compare Rental Property Tools"` passes through the template to become `"Veld Alternatives — Compare Rental Property Tools | Veld Portfolio"` — approximately 67 characters. "Veld" appears twice. This will typically be truncated in Google SERPs (~55–60 display chars on desktop) and the brand suffix adds noise.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/alternatives/page.tsx` line 15.

---

**F-M4 — Breadcrumb navigation on calculator tool pages has no `BreadcrumbList` JSON-LD**
`/tools/brrr`, `/tools/str-vs-ltr`, and `/tools/fix-and-flip` each render a visible `<nav aria-label="Breadcrumb">` with Calculators → [Tool Name] but emit no machine-readable `BreadcrumbList` schema. Google could display path breadcrumbs in SERPs for these tool pages (replacing the raw URL), improving CTR on high-value calculator URLs.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/tools/brrr/page.tsx` lines 45–51; `app/app/tools/str-vs-ltr/page.tsx` lines 46–52; `app/app/tools/fix-and-flip/page.tsx` lines 45–51. No JSON-LD breadcrumb component used.

---

**F-M5 — `/lp/` variant route not covered in `robots.txt` disallow**
`/lp/investment-property-calculator` correctly sets `robots: { index: false, follow: true }` and points its canonical to `/investment-property-calculator`. However, `robots.ts` does not disallow `/lp/`. Googlebot will crawl the page, read the noindex, and respect it — so there is no indexing risk — but crawl budget is spent on the LP variant unnecessarily. Disallowing `/lp/` at the robots level would be cleaner.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/robots.ts` (no `/lp/` in disallow list); `app/app/lp/investment-property-calculator/page.tsx` line 19.

---

**F-M6 — Twitter card metadata not overridden on any public page**
All pages inherit root-layout Twitter metadata: same title, description, and image from `app/app/layout.tsx` lines 63–76. High-signal pages (pricing, tools hub, BRRRR calculator) all show identical Twitter cards. Pages that do set page-level `openGraph` metadata don't also set `twitter` metadata, so the Twitter card does not pick up the page-specific OG content.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/layout.tsx` lines 63–76 (global Twitter fallback); review of all public `page.tsx` files — none set `twitter` in their page `metadata` export.

---

**F-M7 — Search Console property verification and sitemap submission not documented**
The process doc (§2.9) flags this as operational; there is no `docs/` entry, `README`, or `.env.example` comment indicating whether Google Search Console has been claimed and the sitemap submitted at `{APP_URL}/sitemap.xml`. Without sitemap submission, discovery of the 50+ location tool pages relies entirely on internal linking.
- **Evidence:** `docs/process/seo-audit-process.md` §2.9; no corroborating documentation found in repo.

---

**F-M8 — `sitemap.ts` uses `new Date()` for all `lastModified` values**
Every entry in the sitemap reports today's date as the last modification time, regardless of whether the content changed. This renders the `lastModified` signal meaningless for crawl prioritization. Crawlers may re-crawl all URLs on every request or learn to ignore the signal entirely.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/sitemap.ts` lines 11, 18, 20, 25, 29 (and throughout) — all use `new Date()`.

### Low

**F-L1 — Home page canonical has trailing slash; all other page canonicals do not**
The home page sets `alternates: { canonical: APP_URL + "/" }` (with trailing slash), while every other page uses `APP_URL + "/pricing"`, `APP_URL + "/tools"` etc. (no trailing slash). Mixed trailing-slash canonicals are not a major risk in Next.js + Vercel (hosting handles normalization) but can create subtle duplication signals if crawled from different entry points.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/page.tsx` line 45 vs. `app/app/pricing/page.tsx` line 23 and all others.

---

**F-L2 — `keywords` metadata used only on changelog; inconsistent across site**
`/changelog` sets a `keywords` array (line 27–33 in `changelog/page.tsx`). No other public page uses `keywords`. Google ignores `keywords` for ranking, but the inconsistency creates signal asymmetry and the values on changelog (e.g. `"changelog"`) are low-value. Either adopt a consistent keywords strategy or remove from changelog.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/changelog/page.tsx` lines 27–33; all other public pages — no `keywords` export.

---

**F-L3 — `/resources` hub title "Resources" is not keyword-rich**
Resolves to `"Resources | Veld Portfolio"` — highly generic for a hub that contains investor math explainers (DSCR, cap rate, BRRRR, etc.). The meta description is well-targeted ("Plain-language explainers on DSCR, cap rate, cash-on-cash…"), but the title is the strongest ranking signal and it currently uses a single generic word.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/resources/page.tsx` line 13.

---

**F-L4 — `/pricing` meta title "Pricing" is brief**
Resolves to `"Pricing | Veld Portfolio"`. Contains no product-differentiating keywords (e.g. "rental portfolio tracker," "real estate analytics"). Competitors targeting "real estate portfolio tracker pricing" or "landlord software pricing" would outrank on title alone.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/pricing/page.tsx` line 19.

---

**F-L5 — Home page H1 / meta title keyword alignment is loose**
Meta title: `"Veld Portfolio — Track Your Rental Properties in One Place"`.
H1: `"Replace spreadsheet chaos with one clear view of your rental portfolio"`.
The title targets "track your rental properties," while the H1 pivots to "replace spreadsheet chaos." Both messages are valid, but from a keyword perspective they don't reinforce the same primary query. Neither contains "portfolio analytics" or "rental property portfolio tracker" — the core category phrase in the OG title (`"Portfolio Analytics for Real Estate Investors"`).
- **Evidence:** `app/app/page.tsx` lines 39–51 (metadata), lines 183–190 (H1).

---

**F-L6 — Footer nav links hidden on mobile via `hidden md:flex`**
The footer's primary navigation (Calculators, Changelog, Alternatives, Compare, Resources) is wrapped in `hidden md:flex`, making it invisible on mobile viewports. Googlebot uses mobile-first indexing. The links are present in the DOM but hidden via CSS — Google will see them, but the page fails to provide a coherent mobile navigation experience, and the mobile-first rendered page lacks visible internal links to key hub pages. This is a potential crawl graph depth issue for mobile-first indexing.
- **Evidence:** `app/components/footer.tsx` line 16 (`hidden ... md:flex`).

---

**F-L7 — `/vs` hub page: no `changeFrequency` differentiation for VS hub vs. VS pages in sitemap**
The `/vs` hub and both `/vs/spreadsheets`, `/vs/excel-rental-property` detail pages all use `changeFrequency: "monthly"` and `priority: 0.8` / `0.85`. These distinction priorities are reasonable but the hub has the same or lower priority than the detail pages, which is semantically backwards — the hub page should have higher priority as the canonical entry point.
- **Evidence:** `app/app/sitemap.ts` lines 75–91.

## Recommendations (prioritized)

1. **Add page-level OG + Twitter images for pricing, tools hub, and top calculators** (addresses F-H1). Even a small set of 3–5 unique 1200×630 images for key landing pages would materially improve social preview quality.
2. **Make sitemap alternatives and vs entries dynamic** (addresses F-H2): replace hardcoded slug arrays with `Object.keys(COMPETITOR_ALTERNATIVES).map(...)` and `Object.keys(COMPETITOR_VS).map(...)`, mirroring the resources pattern.
3. **Add `FAQPage` JSON-LD to `/pricing`** (addresses F-H3): use the existing `CalculatorFaqJsonLd` component pattern or a shared FAQ JSON-LD utility. The 4 Q&A items are well-suited for rich results.
4. **Fix `/vs` hub metadata title** (addresses F-M2): change from `"Compare"` to something like `"Veld vs Spreadsheets — Rental Portfolio Comparison"` so SERP display title and H1 reinforce the same keyword.
5. **Add `BreadcrumbList` JSON-LD to tool calculator pages** (addresses F-M4): a small shared component can emit the schema for any page that renders a visible breadcrumb trail.
6. **Document Search Console setup** (addresses F-M7): add a `docs/ops/search-console.md` or `.env.example` note confirming sitemap submission at `{APP_URL}/sitemap.xml`.
7. **Switch `lastModified` to static dates per content type** (addresses F-M8): use a hardcoded date constant tied to content deploy, or omit `lastModified` entirely rather than always emitting today's date.
8. **Add `/lp/` to robots.ts disallow** (addresses F-M5): `disallow: "/lp/"` prevents crawl budget spend on noindex LP variants.
