Section 1

Executive Summary

The overhaul successfully addressed the three core problems identified in the original assessment: no brand color identity, a card-heavy layout that created visual noise, and a marketing site that felt generic and unconvincing. The indigo accent system is live and cohesive. The card proliferation in Settings and property detail tabs has been resolved through panel consolidation. The landing page has been fundamentally rebuilt.

The product reads demonstrably more modern on both the marketing side and in-app. The design system has been documented and is enforceable. Key gaps remain — primarily in workspaces not yet touched (Modeling, Mortgage, Plans billing page) and in the lack of micro-interaction polish — but these are refinement-level concerns, not structural ones.

✅

Net result: The site moved from a functional-but-dated internal tool aesthetic to a modern, consistent SaaS product. The gap between the product's quality and how the marketing site presented it has been closed.

Section 3

Landing Page

Before
  • Text-only hero, no product screenshot
  • Generic value props with no visual anchors
  • No "how it works" sequence
  • Pricing teaser was a plain text link
  • No social proof or trust signals
After
  • Asymmetric 60/40 hero with product screenshot on lg+
  • Social proof strip with user/review count signals
  • Value props have brand-tinted icon containers
  • "How it works" 3-step numbered sequence
  • Elevated calculator section mid-page

What's Working Well

The hero is now doing the job a SaaS hero should do: showing the product immediately rather than asking the visitor to imagine it. The social proof strip creates credibility at a point in the funnel where visitors are deciding whether to read further. The "How it works" section answers the most common visitor question ("What do I actually do with this?") without requiring them to sign up first.

Remaining Concerns

  • Medium
    The social proof numbers (user count, review count) are placeholder. They should be replaced with real data or removed entirely — fabricated social proof erodes trust if a visitor investigates.
  • Low
    The hero screenshot is a static asset that now shows the pre-overhaul design. It should be retaken to reflect the new indigo accent system and panel layouts.
  • Low
    The calculator CTA section mid-page is elevated but uses the same visual weight as the surrounding sections. A subtle background differentiation (e.g. bg-accent/5 section) would better signal it as a tool, not just content.
Section 4

Pricing Page

Before
  • Plain circular bullet points instead of checkmarks
  • No visual hierarchy between tiers
  • Annual toggle had indigo + green badge clash when selected
  • Fine print split across two orphaned paragraphs
  • No trust signals below the header
After
  • Lucide Check icons for feature lists
  • Recommended tier has indigo ring + tinted background
  • Annual toggle badge hidden when already selected (fixed today)
  • Fine print consolidated into one paragraph; link style softened
  • Trust pill chips below header; screenshot gallery below cards

What's Working Well

The tier hierarchy is now clear. A visitor scanning the pricing page at a glance knows which plan is recommended without reading all the copy. The screenshots below the cards give unauthenticated visitors the same evidence as the landing page. The FAQ section with hover-reveal answers reduces pre-purchase friction.

Remaining Concerns

  • Low
    When annual billing is selected, the annual price displays in text-positive (green). This is semantic (savings = positive), but the same green is used for "Current plan" badges and check icons on the same page. A single green carrying three distinct meanings can feel slightly confused. Consider using the accent color for the selected annual price instead.
  • Low
    The "See it in action" screenshots section still shows the older dashboard design. Should be refreshed when new screenshots are available.
Section 5

In-App Navigation

Before
  • Flat ungrouped list of nav items
  • "Add property" was a ghost link at the bottom
  • Active state was just a subtle background change
  • No visual section separation
After
  • Three groups: Portfolio / Tools / Account with labels
  • "Add property" is an accent-tinted button with Plus icon
  • Active state has left accent bar (indigo border-l-2)
  • Font size tightened for more compact feel

What's Working Well

The grouping now makes the nav scannable in under a second. A user knows where to find analytics tools vs. account settings without reading every link. The "Add property" button calling visual attention to itself at the bottom aligns with the primary user action.

ℹ️

Looking at the desktop screenshot at 1920×1080, the sidebar reads well. The indigo active bar on "Dashboard" is the clearest active-state indicator the app has had. The section labels (PORTFOLIO, TOOLS, ACCOUNT) at text-xs uppercase are appropriately muted.

Remaining Concerns

  • Low
    The sidebar logo mark at 20×20px is on the small side for 1920px wide displays. The Veld wordmark next to it compensates, but a 24×24px mark would feel more intentional at larger viewports.
  • Low
    The "Deals" nav item sits under Tools, which is semantically accurate but may feel unexpected to users who think of deals as part of their portfolio tracking, not tooling. Worth monitoring with user feedback.
Section 6

Dashboard

Before
  • Action area was a card with the same visual weight as metric cards
  • No visual grouping on primary metric row
  • Empty state had no illustration, just text
  • Single-property upsell was plain and unbranded
  • Mobile: workspace links were heavy full-width stacked buttons
After
  • Action strip is a plain flex row — no card wrapper
  • Primary metric grid sits in a subtle bg-subtle/30 container
  • Empty state has illustration + progressive disclosure for other options
  • Upsell card is accent-tinted (indigo border/bg)
  • Mobile: workspace links are chip-style in a contained quiet panel

What's Working Well

The desktop view (as seen in the provided 1920×1080 screenshot) reads significantly cleaner. The primary metrics, "More metrics," Rent vs Market, and Portfolio charts sections each have distinct visual weight and spacing. The action buttons at the top are now clearly a utility strip, not content.

The metric grouping container does what it was designed to do: it tells the eye "these five things belong together" without adding a border or shadow that would make it feel like another card.

Remaining Concerns

  • Medium
    The secondary metric row (LTV, NOI, Cash-on-cash, Annual rent, DSCR) — visible via "More metrics" expansion on mobile and always visible on desktop — does not have the same subtle grouping container as the primary metrics. On desktop they render as raw individual cards without a container, creating a visual inconsistency between the two rows.
  • Medium
    At 1920×1080, the main content area is constrained to a medium width by the sidebar, leaving noticeable white space on the right. This is intentional for readability, but could be addressed with a slightly wider max-width or a two-column layout for the metrics + Rent vs. Market section on very wide screens.
  • Low
    The "Portfolio charts" section header and chart tab buttons are slightly disconnected from the chart panel visually. Wrapping the tab bar and the chart in a single panel (with the tab bar as a header strip inside it) would reinforce that they're one interactive widget, not two separate elements.
  • Low
    The Rent vs. Market section retains a bg-card rounded-xl border panel but the "RENT VS. MARKET" label inside is still uppercase-muted. This is one of the few remaining places where the old uppercase section title pattern persists in a prominent spot.
Section 7

Properties & Property Detail

Before
  • Property cards had no hover feedback
  • Tab content had multiple sibling cards per tab
  • Back navigation was plain arrow text
  • Property hero address block used bg-card
After
  • Cards have hover:shadow-md transition-shadow
  • Tab content consolidated into single panels with internal dividers
  • Back link uses ChevronLeft icon, styled consistently with contact page
  • Property hero uses bg-subtle for de-emphasis

What's Working Well

The tab content consolidation is one of the most impactful changes in the whole overhaul. Moving from 3–4 separate cards per tab to a single panel with internal dividers removes the repetitive card borders that made the page feel like an accordion of boxes. The content reads as one coherent object now.

Remaining Concerns

  • Low
    The property list page at very small property counts (1–2 properties) renders a large single-card view that is spacious but possibly over-padded compared to the tighter multi-property grid view. The card sizes feel inconsistent between these two states.

  • 6 separate cards with equal visual weight
  • <h2> headings that functioned as labels inside cards
  • Delete account used md:border-border, hiding danger styling on desktop

Settings is the clearest win in the overhaul. The page went from visually noisy (six equal-weight boxes) to structured (four purposeful panels). This is exactly what the Surface Hierarchy system was designed to solve.

Section 9

Supporting Public Pages

Changelog

The timeline treatment (vertical rail, accent dots, date chips) transformed what was a plain bulleted list into something that actually looks like a product changelog. It signals active development and gives each release a clear visual anchor. This is a high-value, low-effort improvement.

Contact

Minor but correct: the back navigation now uses the ChevronLeft icon and matches the property detail back-link pattern. Consistency in micro-navigation patterns is easy to overlook and easy to spot when it's wrong.

Footer

Reorganised from a flat list into two rows: product/navigation links and legal/support links. This is a cleaner structure and aligns the footer with how SaaS footers typically organize themselves. The copyright anchor on the left at wider viewports is a good visual separation.

Remaining Concerns

  • Low
    The /tools (public calculators hub) and individual calculator pages (/tools/deal-analyzer, etc.) have not been assessed in this overhaul. These are public-facing pages that unauthenticated users see and should eventually receive the same polish as the pricing and landing pages.
Section 10

Design System Consistency

This is where the overhaul achieves something more durable than aesthetic changes: it establishes an enforceable, documented design language for the first time.

What's in Place

  • Done
    Token layer — CSS variables for accent (indigo), accent-subtle, accent-hover, plus all existing semantic tokens. Tailwind @theme inline mappings are consistent.
  • Done
    Surface Hierarchy — Three formal levels (Page / Panel / Inset) documented in both the design brief and the implementation guide with exact Tailwind class strings.
  • Done
    Typography roles — Section titles are no longer the old uppercase-muted pattern in any of the touched pages. Level 1 (page title), Level 2 (section nav), Level 3 (panel internal label) roles are clear.
  • Done
    Shadow system — shadow-sm consistently applied to Raised surfaces (cards, panels). Elevated (shadow-md) is reserved for hover states. Floating (shadow-lg) is reserved for modals and dropdowns.
  • Done
    Radius system — rounded-xl for panels, rounded-lg for nested insets, rounded-md for buttons and badges. Nested radius principle applied consistently.
  • Done
    Clerk appearance — Sign-in / sign-up flows now use the indigo primary color, matching the app's brand instead of Clerk's default blue.
  • Done
    design-spec.md updated — The legacy spec file now references the new design brief for superseded sections and the accent table has been updated to indigo values.

Remaining Gaps in the System

  • Medium
    The animation/transition layer is almost entirely absent. Page transitions, skeleton loading states, and micro-interactions are currently handled ad-hoc (or not at all). A small animation token set (duration-150, duration-200, ease-out) and a defined use policy would prevent future inconsistency as new features are built.
  • Low
    The empty-state pattern has been applied to Dashboard (empty-properties.png) and Deals (empty-deals.png) but is not yet documented as a formal pattern in the design brief. Other zero-states (no deals saved, no modeling runs) may be plain text only.
  • Low
    The form input style is not explicitly reviewed or updated in this overhaul. Given the indigo accent, focus rings (ring-accent/20) on inputs may not have been uniformly updated across all form pages (Add property, mortgage inputs, deal analyzer).
Section 11

Pages Not Yet Addressed

The following in-app pages were outside the scope of this overhaul and still reflect the pre-overhaul design language. They function correctly but will feel slightly inconsistent when a user navigates to them from the updated pages.

  • Medium
    Modeling workspace (/modeling) — scenario projections, charts, and assumption inputs. This is a high-value page for paid users and likely has the same card-heavy structure as the old property detail page did.
  • Medium
    Mortgage workspace (/mortgage) — payoff modeling and projection charts. Frequently linked from the dashboard workspace strip. Pre-overhaul header, card, and section patterns.
  • Medium
    Plans & billing (/plans) — where signed-in users actually manage subscriptions. This is the in-app counterpart to the pricing page and should receive the same tier emphasis and panel treatment.
  • Low
    Public calculator pages (/tools/*) — individual calculator tools accessible without an account. These are SEO-valuable pages that potential users see before signing up.
  • Low
    Admin page (/admin) — internal only, lowest priority.

Section 13

Overall Verdict

7.5 / 10
Post-implementation overall score
Up from approximately 4.5 / 10 pre-overhaul. The product is now competitive with similarly-priced SaaS tools in the real estate analytics space. The gap between the quality of the underlying data and the quality of the presentation has been substantially closed.

What Made the Biggest Difference

In order of visual impact:

  1. Indigo accent system — gave the product a distinct, ownable color identity it did not have before. Every CTA, active state, and icon accent now reads as intentional.
  2. Landing page rebuild — the marketing site went from "this could be any SaaS tool" to "this is specifically a real estate portfolio product, here's what it looks like."
  3. Settings and property detail panel consolidation — removed the most egregious examples of card overuse. These pages now feel like a product, not a form builder.
  4. Navigation grouping and active state — the sidebar went from a flat link list to a structured navigation. Users can now orient themselves instantly.

The Honest Gaps

The product still lacks the micro-interaction polish that differentiates a 7.5 from a 9. There are no page transition animations, skeleton loading states are minimal, and the form input layer hasn't been audited. The Modeling and Mortgage workspaces — which paid users spend the most time in — still have the pre-overhaul aesthetic. These are the next frontier.

📋

For the next sprint, the single highest-ROI action is updating the product screenshots on the landing page and pricing page. The marketing site now promises a modern product, but the screenshots still show the old one. That disconnect is the most visible remaining issue for new visitors.
