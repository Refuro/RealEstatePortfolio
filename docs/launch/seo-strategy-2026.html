Part 1 — What Changed in SEO

The 2026 landscape is fundamentally different

🤖
AI Overviews dominate search
Google AI Overviews appear on ~85% of informational queries. Position #1 is now visually position #5 — pushed 1,200px down on desktop. The goal shifts from ranking to being cited inside the AI answer.
📄
Blog posts convert very poorly
Educational blog posts convert at 0.1–0.3% to free trial. Competitor comparison and tool pages convert at 2–5%. The traditional "blog for SEO" playbook is one of the weakest SaaS strategies in 2026.
⚡
Programmatic pages win for SaaS
Companies generating 100–2,400 data-driven pages at scale are seeing 250K–16M monthly visitors. These pages target long-tail queries (50–500 searches/mo each) that are impossible to hit manually at volume.
🎯
Bottom-of-funnel intent is gold
The 2026 hierarchy inverts: start with alternatives, comparisons, and use-case calculators — not top-of-funnel education. These pages are closer to the buy decision and convert far higher.
🧱
Structured data is mandatory
LLMs and crawlers extract structured, machine-readable content. FAQ JSON-LD, schema markup, clear H-tags, tables, and comparison formats are how you become a cited source — not just a ranked page.
🌐
New surfaces: ChatGPT, Perplexity
Users now discover products via ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude. These systems cite data-rich, authoritative sources. Being structured and factual matters more than being first to publish.

Known gaps from your own audit (2026-04-01)

Medium risk

FAQ JSON-LD not mirrored as visible HTML

All 4 calculator pages emit FAQPage structured data but the questions aren't rendered as visible page content. Google's guidelines require this match. Rich result eligibility is at risk. This is already fixed per tasks.md — CalculatorFaqSection is wired.

Medium risk

Canonical origin normalization split

Some page-level canonicals used raw NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL instead of getAppOrigin(). A trailing slash in env could produce //tools double-slash URLs. Also already fixed per tasks.md.

Gap

"Spreadsheets vs Veld", "Stessa alternative", "Rentastic alternative" — these convert at 2–5%. You're invisible on this entire intent type, which is where people in the consideration stage search.

Gap

Part 5 — Blog: Honest Assessment

Should you build a blog?

⚠️

The standard argument for a blog is weaker in 2026 than it has ever been

AI Overviews now absorb the vast majority of informational blog queries — "how does DSCR work", "what is cash-on-cash return", "BRRRR method explained" — all of these now get answered directly in Google without a click. You'd be writing content that feeds Google's AI more than it drives visits to your site.

On top of that, you're competing with BiggerPockets, Investopedia, and NerdWallet — sites with massive domain authority and existing content on every real estate topic. Writing generic blog posts would not yield meaningful traffic for 12–18 months minimum, if ever.

❌ Blog posts that won't work

  • • "What is cash-on-cash return?" — AI Overview wins
  • • "How to analyze a rental property" — BiggerPockets wins
  • • "BRRRR method explained" — 1,000 articles exist
  • • "Best real estate investment strategies 2026" — AI/aggregator wins
  • • Generic market reports with no proprietary data

✅ If you blog, only write these

  • • "How Veld calculates equity vs other trackers" — product-led
  • • "We tracked 50 BRRRR deals — here's what the math showed" — original data
  • • "Migrating from Stessa to Veld: what to expect" — high-converting
  • • Changelog-style product deep dives — trust building
  • • Customer case studies (with real numbers) — social proof
🏁 Verdict: A blog is not the right primary investment. If you write anything, make it product-led or data-driven — content only you could publish. Never write generic real estate education.

Part 6 — Final Recommendation

Extend the calculator engine — don't start a blog
Your biggest competitive advantage is working, free, production-quality calculators. Scale them with programmatic pages and comparison content. That's the 2026 playbook.
  1. 1
    Build 3–5 competitor alternative pages Quick win · 1–2 weeks
    "/alternatives/stessa", "/vs/spreadsheets", "/alternatives/rentastic". These are the highest-converting pages in SaaS SEO. One feature comparison table + embedded calculator CTA = done. These pages reach people at the moment of choosing a tool.
  2. 2
    Add location-based calculator variants Programmatic · 4–6 weeks
    Start with top 10–15 high-investor-density states (TX, FL, CA, AZ, GA, NC, TN, OH, CO, WA). Build a location data file + template. Each page uses the same calculator component, adds a local context section (avg rents, market cap rates if sourceable), and links internally. 50 pages in one build cycle.
  3. 3
    Surface calculators in investor communities Free distribution · Ongoing
    Reddit (r/realestateinvesting, r/landlord, r/financialindependence), BiggerPockets, local REIA forums. Share the BRRRR or deal calculator in threads where people are doing deal math. These links rank in Google now and drive warm, qualified traffic. Your launch plan already names these communities.
  4. 4
    Add a /resources section (not a blog) Medium term · 2–4 weeks
    Not a blog — a structured data reference hub. "DSCR explained + calculator", "Cap rate benchmarks by market", "Rental yield calculator". Structured content Google and AI can cite. Links to your tools. Think reference pages, not articles. These are citation-ready for AI Overviews.
  5. 5
    Verify Search Console + fix any open indexing gaps Foundation · 1 day
    Your seo-release-checklist.md already has this: verify Google Search Console property, submit sitemap, confirm NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL has no trailing slash in Vercel. Run Rich Results Test on one calculator. These are owner steps but need to happen before any new pages have impact.
